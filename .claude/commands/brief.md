# Brief

1. Identify the reader's decision and intended scope. When asked to read all, enumerate the sources and track what you actually opened.
2. Read the original sources and extract the decisive facts, dates, figures, and disagreement or missing evidence that changes the answer.
3. Lead with the conclusion. Pick the three to five points the reader needs and attach a primary URL to each factual point where available. Use the vocabulary of the audience's work.
4. State what you did and what remains only when either affects the result. Preserve the distinction between reported facts, estimates, and your interpretation.
